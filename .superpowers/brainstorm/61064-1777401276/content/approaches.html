Three approaches — pick the shape, then we detail it

All three share the same back-end goal: every signal captured during the walkthrough writes back to the project record. They differ in how the surface accommodates variable pace (some projects "nothing new", others spawn a 10-min discussion).

A. Inline-amplified card

Stay close to today's UI. Add per-card prompt strip ("amber on schedule — log a mitigation?"), inline +Decision / +Action / +Risk / +Comms buttons, and a clear "last_reviewed" stamp written to the project. Same card height as today.

Best for: minimal UX disruption, fastest to ship, low learning curve.
Trade-off: deep capture still feels like a form on a card.

B. Two-mode card (Skim / Deep)

Variable pace by design. Default = collapsed one-liner with RAG+status+Done button → ~10 sec per project. Click to expand → full capture with prompt strip, inline decisions/actions/risk add, chip progress, "what changed since last week". One project deep at a time.

Best for: live meetings with mixed pace; matches your "10 sec for some, 10 min for others" reality.
Trade-off: two states to design + animate; slightly more complex than A.

C. Conversation timeline

Project as a feed. Each project shows a reverse-chronological timeline of last week's events (RAG changes, risks, audit) with a single capture box at the bottom. Type "@alice send slides by Friday" → action filed. Type "decision: park phase 2" → decision logged.

Best for: a "feed" mental model — appending to the project's narrative.
Trade-off: highest design complexity (parsing free-text capture is brittle); biggest departure from today's UI.

My recommendation: B (Two-mode card). It maps directly to your "variable pace per project" reality — most projects skim in 10 seconds, some open up for a real conversation. It builds on the card UX you already have rather than starting over (C), and it's where the prompt-strip / "ask the right question" idea naturally lives (the prompts surface in Deep mode where there's room).
